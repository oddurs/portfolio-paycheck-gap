When markets compound faster than paychecks

PPG compares the growth of a broad US stock-market investment with the growth of the median full-time paycheck. It measures relative paths—not a dollar gap, valuation, or typical worker portfolio.

01

Start at 100

Both components equal 100 in 1980 Q1. The base is a measuring reference, not a claim about fairness.

02

Compare growth

Above 100 means market wealth grew faster; below 100 means the paycheck grew faster over the same span.

03

Keep claims narrow

PPG does not show who owns stocks, explain causation, measure living costs, or forecast either series.

The populations are different.

The market leg is a hypothetical investment. The paycheck leg is a changing cross-sectional median for full-time wage and salary workers. PPG does not imply that those workers own the portfolio.
