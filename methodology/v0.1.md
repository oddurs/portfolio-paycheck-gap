# Portfolio–Paycheck Gap Index methodology v0.1

Status: accepted

Effective: 2026-09-13

Base quarter: 1980 Q1

Frequency: quarterly

## 1. Purpose

The Portfolio–Paycheck Gap Index (PPG) measures how the cumulative return to a
representative US-listed stock-market investment has changed relative to the
median full-time paycheck.

PPG compares growth factors, not dollar levels. A value of 150 means that since
the base quarter the market growth factor is 1.5 times the median-paycheck
growth factor. It does not mean stocks are 50% overvalued, workers are 50%
underpaid, or the base period was an equilibrium.

The [core-definition decision](decisions/0005-core-definition.md) supports the
question, population boundaries, alignment, inflation treatment, precision,
and interpretation in this specification.

## 2. Scope

### Market concept

The ideal market leg is the gross total return of a passive, broad US public
equity portfolio: regular distributions reinvested, no leverage or external
cash flow, and before investor taxes, fees, transaction costs, or tracking
error.

The open v0.1 proxy is JKP's value-weighted portfolio of common, primary
securities on main US exchanges. It is weighted by lagged market equity rather
than free float and classifies the United States by exchange country rather
than issuer domicile. The difference from the ideal is a disclosed source
limitation, not a redefinition of the ideal.

### Paycheck concept

The paycheck leg is the seasonally adjusted quarterly median of usual weekly
earnings for civilian US wage and salary workers age 16 or older who usually
work at least 35 hours per week at their sole or principal job. Earnings are in
current dollars before taxes and deductions and include usual overtime,
commissions, and tips at that job.

It excludes part-time and self-employed workers, employer benefits, capital
income, transfers, secondary-job earnings, household size, and people without
wage or salary work. It is a changing cross-sectional median, not a worker
panel.

### Claims not supported

PPG is not a valuation, market-timing, recession, or return forecast; a
cost-of-living or affordability index; household wealth or retirement
readiness; a complete income/wealth inequality measure; labor share,
productivity, or compensation; or a claim about a socially optimal level.

## 3. Canonical sources

The exact source contract and rights analysis are in the [data-source
decision](decisions/0006-data-sources.md).

### Equity excess return

- Publisher: Jensen, Kelly, and Pedersen, Global Factor Data.
- Selection: `usa / mkt / monthly / vw`.
- Endpoint:
  `https://jkpfactors-data.s3.amazonaws.com/public/%5Busa%5D_%5Bmkt%5D_%5Bmonthly%5D_%5Bvw%5D.zip`.
- Value: `ret`, decimal monthly US-dollar excess return.
- Accepted-vintage coverage: 1926-01 through 2025-12.
- Cadence: irregular publisher refresh; no fixed release calendar.

### Treasury proxy

- Publisher: Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, H.15.
- Series: `H15/H15/RIFSGFSM03_N.M`.
- Delivery: FRED `TB3MS` CSV at
  `https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=TB3MS`.
- Value: monthly average 3-month Treasury bill secondary-market discount rate,
  percent per year, not seasonally adjusted.
- Coverage: 1934-01 onward.
- Cadence: monthly H.15 average, revised when the publisher revises it.

### Median paycheck

- Publisher: US Bureau of Labor Statistics, Current Population Survey.
- Series: `LES1252881500`.
- Endpoint:
  `https://api.bls.gov/publicAPI/v2/timeseries/data/LES1252881500` with explicit
  `startyear` and `endyear`.
- Value: quarterly seasonally adjusted median usual weekly nominal earnings,
  current US dollars.
- Coverage: 1979 Q1 onward.
- Cadence: normally released in the month after quarter end; five years of
  seasonally adjusted history may be revised annually.

The headline publication range begins in 1979 Q1, the first paycheck quarter,
provided every required monthly input exists. The accepted source vintage's
latest mutually complete quarter is 2025 Q3; BLS did not produce 2025 Q4.

## 4. Calculation

### 4.1 Normalize monthly inputs

Parse source numbers without rounding. Map each market and Treasury observation
to its `YYYY-MM` calendar month. Require exactly one of each for every month in
the publication range. Sort ascending after detecting duplicates.

For month `m`, let `e_m` be JKP decimal excess return, `y_m` be H.15 discount
yield in percent per year, `D_m` be calendar days in the month, and `N = 91`
days. Calculate:

```text
bill_price_m = 1 - (y_m / 100) × (N / 360)
rf_proxy_m   = bill_price_m ^ (-D_m / N) - 1
market_ret_m = e_m + rf_proxy_m
```

Require `0 < bill_price_m <= 1` and `market_ret_m > -1`. The Treasury term is a
transparent approximation, not the exact one-month risk-free series subtracted
by JKP. It assumes a 91-day bill and smooth accrual and ignores mark-to-market
and term-structure effects.

### 4.2 Build quarter-end market wealth

Set an arbitrary wealth value `M_0 = 1` immediately before the first included
market month. For every month in order:

```text
M_m = M_(m-1) × (1 + market_ret_m)
```

For quarter `t`, `M_t` is wealth after the March, June, September, or December
return. Compounding—not summing—combines returns. The date is the calendar
month-end label for the final eligible market observation.

### 4.3 Normalize quarterly paycheck

Map BLS `Q01`–`Q04` to `YYYY-Q1`–`YYYY-Q4`. Parse a numeric `value` as current
weekly dollars. Transform the BLS `-` placeholder to missing; never to zero.
Do not interpolate, extrapolate, average across adjacent quarters, or carry a
paycheck forward.

### 4.4 Align and rebase

Join market wealth and paycheck on calendar quarter. Publish only complete
pairs. Let `b = 1980-Q1`, with market value `M_b` and paycheck value `W_b`.

```text
MARKET_t   = 100 × M_t / M_b
PAYCHECK_t = 100 × W_t / W_b
PPG_t      = 100 × MARKET_t / PAYCHECK_t
```

The implementation must also calculate the direct equivalent and require
agreement before storage:

```text
PPG_t = 100 × (M_t / M_b) / (W_t / W_b)
```

The arbitrary initial market wealth and the base scale cancel from relative
movements. Deflating both components by the same positive, unrounded price
index also cancels exactly; the headline therefore uses nominal inputs.

### 4.5 Precision

Use IEEE 754 binary64 or higher precision. Source inputs and intermediates are
unrounded. Serialize the two components and PPG to six decimal places using
round-to-nearest, ties-to-even. Display PPG and percentage changes to one
decimal place, calculated from unrounded values. Values within `0.0000005`
index point are equal at canonical storage precision.

## 5. Validation and failure behavior

The [sensitivity and failure-policy decision](sensitivity-analysis.md) is
normative. In summary:

- fail on missing historical periods, duplicates, invalid source identity,
  missing required fields, nonfinite/nonpositive levels, a monthly return at or
  below -1, or an unsupported Treasury quote;
- transform explicit missing markers to null and sort valid unsorted rows;
- warn on extra source fields and ignore them by name; and
- if only the newest quarter is incomplete, retain the prior reading and label
  it stale rather than failing or inventing a current value.

A new archive checksum is quarantined. Compare old and new history, explain the
change, recompute every affected PPG row, and obtain review before publication.
The accepted JKP migration changed its rebased 2023 Q4 component by 2.229920%;
small monthly changes can compound materially.

## 6. Publication and provenance

Every published dataset exposes the component paths as well as PPG. A release
includes a companion metadata record containing methodology version,
generation time, latest complete quarter, source retrieval times/checksums,
and stale status. The normative field contract is the [v0.1 data
dictionary](data-dictionary-v0.1.md).

Publication must not claim freshness beyond the newest complete JKP/BLS pair.
If a source is unavailable, a verified cached vintage can reproduce an old
release but cannot support a newly retrieved label.

## 8. Independent implementation checklist

An implementation conforms to v0.1 only if it:

1. retrieves the three exact series in section 3 and records their vintages;
2. validates identifiers, units, frequency, schema, uniqueness, and coverage;
3. applies the monthly Treasury formula with calendar-day counts and 91 days;
4. adds that proxy to JKP excess return and compounds monthly gross returns;
5. selects March/June/September/December ending wealth;
6. joins the numeric BLS quarterly observations without filling gaps;
7. rebases both components to 1980 Q1 and checks the direct-ratio invariant;
8. rounds only when serializing the fields declared in the data dictionary;
9. reproduces [`reference-calculation.csv`](../tests/fixtures/reference-calculation.csv)
   byte for byte; and
10. applies the failure, revision, attribution, and stale-reading rules above.

The reference fixture must produce PPG `100.000000`, `114.257990`, and
`124.847450` for 1980 Q1–Q3. `make methodology-check` executes the reference
notebook and all v0.1 methodology assertions without network access.

## 9. Limitations

The index compares an investable-market concept with a worker statistic that
describes a different population. It does not measure who owns equities. The
open market proxy differs from a float-adjusted, domicile-screened benchmark;
its total return is reconstructed with an approximate risk-free leg; and its
publisher refreshes irregularly. CPS medians have sampling error, grouped-data
estimation effects, composition changes, and revisions. Quarter-end wealth and
quarter-average pay intentionally use different within-quarter summaries.

These limitations constrain interpretation. They do not justify hidden
adjustments, qualitative valuation bands, or causal claims.
